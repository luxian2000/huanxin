## Quantum Computing Application Scenarios for DeepMIMO Data

This section identifies wireless-communication scenarios where (1) classical machine-learning approaches still face fundamental limitations and quantum algorithms are expected to provide improvements, and (2) the models can be directly trained on data generated by **DeepMIMO** (i.e., realistic 5G/6G channel matrices **H** and their associated metadata).

---

### Scenario 1 — CSI Feedback Compression (Quantum Autoencoder)

**Classical limitation:**  
Deep-learning codecs such as CsiNet encode the channel matrix into a fixed-size latent vector.  As the compression ratio increases (e.g., 1/32 or 1/64), classical autoencoders suffer from rapid degradation in reconstruction fidelity because the latent space dimension falls below the intrinsic information content of the channel.  Model size also grows super-linearly when trying to recover quality at high compression ratios.

**Why quantum algorithms are expected to help:**  
A quantum autoencoder encodes the compressed representation into the amplitudes of an *n*-qubit state, giving a Hilbert space of dimension 2^n.  This exponential scaling allows a small number of physical qubits to represent the same information as a much larger classical bottleneck layer.  Parameterised quantum circuits (PQC / StronglyEntanglingLayers) then perform the decoding in this exponentially large space.

**DeepMIMO trainability:** ✅  
DeepMIMO generates channel matrices **H** ∈ ℂ^(N_r × N_t × N_sub) for configurable antenna arrays and subcarrier counts.  These are reshaped into (2, 32, 32) image tensors (real/imaginary parts) and used directly as training targets, exactly as in `QAE083.py` and `QCNN01_gpu.py`.

**Implemented in this repo:** `QAE08.py`, `QAE081.py`, `QAE082.py`, `QAE083.py`, `QAE083_gpu.py`, `QCNN01_gpu.py`

---

### Scenario 2 — Quantum Reservoir Computing for Temporal CSI Prediction

**Classical limitation:**  
Time-series models such as LSTM and GRU are used to predict how the channel **H**(*t*) evolves over time (e.g., for mobility-aware beamforming).  They suffer from vanishing/exploding gradients for long sequences and require large memory to capture non-Markovian dependencies in the channel's temporal dynamics.

**Why quantum algorithms are expected to help:**  
Quantum Reservoir Computing (QRC) maps input data into an exponentially large Hilbert space via a fixed (untrained) quantum circuit, then trains only a small linear readout layer.  The quantum reservoir's intrinsic entanglement provides rich non-linear memory with far fewer trainable parameters than a classical recurrent network.  The Discrete Time Crystal (DTC) variant (`pg08_QRC.py`) exploits period-doubling dynamics to encode temporal correlations.

**DeepMIMO trainability:** ✅  
Sequences of channel snapshots generated by DeepMIMO for a moving user trajectory can be fed as the time-series input.  Each snapshot (after flattening to a 2560-dimensional vector) becomes one time step for the reservoir.  The model in `pg08_QRC.py` already loads `CSI_channel_30km.npy` (a DeepMIMO-derived dataset) for this purpose.

**Implemented in this repo:** `pg08.py`, `pg08_QRC.py`

---

### Scenario 3 — Beamforming Codebook Selection (Quantum Optimisation)

**Classical limitation:**  
Selecting the optimal beam from a large 5G NR codebook is an NP-hard combinatorial optimisation problem.  Classical exhaustive search is intractable for large antenna arrays (e.g., 64 × 64 UPA), and DNN-based predictors generalise poorly to unseen environments because the mapping from channel to optimal beam is highly non-convex.

**Why quantum algorithms are expected to help:**  
Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm (QAOA) and Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) can explore the discrete solution space via quantum superposition and interference, potentially finding near-optimal beams in polynomial circuit depth.  Quantum annealing reformulations of the codebook-selection problem have demonstrated advantages on small instances.

**DeepMIMO trainability:** ✅  
DeepMIMO provides both the channel **H** and the corresponding ground-truth optimal beam index for a given codebook.  This (channel, beam-label) pair constitutes a supervised training sample.  The channel features fed into the quantum classifier/optimiser are identical in format to those used for CSI compression.

---

### Scenario 4 — Channel Estimation via Quantum Kernel Methods

**Classical limitation:**  
Pilot-based MMSE estimators are optimal under Gaussian assumptions but fail in non-stationary, doubly-dispersive channels.  DNN-based estimators require very large training sets to generalise across SNR levels, Doppler spreads and delay profiles.

**Why quantum algorithms are expected to help:**  
Quantum Support Vector Machines (QSVM) and quantum kernel ridge regression can implicitly compute inner products in exponentially large feature spaces via the quantum kernel trick.  The sample complexity for learning in this large feature space can be lower than for a classical DNN of equivalent expressiveness, particularly when the channel's statistical structure is amenable to quantum feature maps.

**DeepMIMO trainability:** ✅  
DeepMIMO can generate pairs of (pilot observations at the receiver, true channel **H**) for any configured scenario.  These pairs directly serve as (input, label) training data for a quantum kernel regression model.

---

### Scenario 5 — User Positioning / Radio Fingerprinting

**Classical limitation:**  
Fingerprint-based localisation trains a classifier that maps a received-signal fingerprint (e.g., a channel vector **h**) to a grid cell or continuous coordinate.  In high-dimensional fingerprint spaces (large antenna arrays), classical classifiers require exponentially many training samples to achieve reliable accuracy (curse of dimensionality).

**Why quantum algorithms are expected to help:**  
Quantum classifiers using amplitude encoding can represent the entire high-dimensional fingerprint in *log*(N) qubits and compute distances in the full Hilbert space.  Quantum metric learning and quantum nearest-neighbour algorithms scale more favourably with fingerprint dimension than their classical counterparts.

**DeepMIMO trainability:** ✅  
DeepMIMO generates channel vectors **h** together with the ground-truth (x, y, z) position of each user.  This (channel-fingerprint, position-label) dataset can train a quantum classifier or regressor directly, without any additional data-generation steps.
